# PRD Quality Review — prd-ai-learning-platform

## Overall verdict

This PRD holds up as the product document of record for the COMP5241 submission: it consolidates the settled initiative baseline without re-deciding it, encodes unusually concrete testable consequences (the 79.96%-vs-80.0% rounding boundary, the 120-second honest-async rule, DST-safe review clocks), states its Non-Goals as real exclusions, and ships counter-metrics that are genuinely counter ("do not optimize"), not decoration. The main risk is that the Success Metrics layer is narrower than the Vision's bet: six of 22 FRs — including the thesis's "spaced return" leg (FR-15) and the instructor functional arc (FR-3, FR-16) — are claimed by no metric, so the integrated-validation story verifies four of the five loop legs the Vision calls "the product." Mechanical issues are minor and listed at the tail.

## Decision-readiness — strong

The PRD is explicit about its consolidation posture — §0: "It consolidates — and does not re-decide — the already-settled product baseline," with each source (R1–R22, D1–D8, AD-1–AD-18) linked and the FR-N ↔ R-N one-to-one claim verified against the initiative file. Decisions are stated as decisions with their provenance: "80% default, instructor-configurable for their courses" (Glossary, D3) is a number with a source, not a "consideration." Trade-offs name what was given up: §1 bounds the claim three ways ("formative learning support, not certified assessment; evidence of learning at a moment, not proof of permanent retention; a heuristic review schedule, not an optimality claim"), and §5 forgoes ten things by name (native mobile, catalog/marketplace, XP/streaks, OCR, efficacy claims, monetization). The seven Open Questions (§10) are genuinely open — provider verification (G2), the pending Node security recheck (G1), narration/isolated-runner feasibility (G4), fixture rights (D8) — none is rhetorical with an answer smuggled into the next sentence. The single `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§9, performance budgets "deliberately unset") sits at a real tension (a pilot needs numbers the PRD refuses to invent) rather than a safe checkpoint. A reader pushing back on any FR finds its settled source cited, not a dodge.

### Findings

None — the dimension does its job.

## Substance over theater — strong

The Vision could not swap into another PRD: "then stays with the learner through the whole arc that courseware normally abandons" is a specific bet, immediately cashed out as "confirmed plan → grounded study → honest assessment → targeted recovery → spaced return, under instructor exact-version approval … is the product, not any single feature" (§1). Differentiation is evidence-backed rather than template furniture: the addendum §A comparables table names nine real products with dates (Q-Chat discontinued June 2025, NotebookLM's FERPA/COPPA stance, Coursebox's "no governed instructor-approval/versioning workflow") and the PRD explicitly refuses the advantage claim ("per C5 it establishes no unique-feature advantage," §8.4). The NFRs are product-specific, not boilerplate — attempt snapshotting at start, "timeout never means success; retries never double-count" (§9), fail-closed integrations — and where a budget would be theater, the PRD says so and gates it instead. The five UJ protagonists each carry a load-bearing encoded decision (Aditi's 79.9% encodes the rounding boundary; Lars's "asked once" encodes the reminder cap; Prof. Chen's retest queue encodes FR-5's no-bypass rule), and §2.3 honestly labels them "illustrative composites … invented for this PRD." No persona roster, no innovation chapter, no copied scalability boilerplate.

### Findings

None.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated and the feature set serves it: §4's six sections each map to one epic, and the MVP slice ("the personal-material learning loop end-to-end," §6.1) is the thesis in miniature, sequenced so every leg exists before the pilot that tests the loop. Counter-metrics are present and correctly oriented — SM-C1 ("Session length … must never be reported or treated as learning evidence"), SM-C2 (no rounding passes into existence), SM-C3 (the reminder cap "is a product promise, not an under-performing lever") — exactly the discipline a one-participant C5 validation needs. What keeps this from strong: the metrics do not reach the whole thesis. The Vision's loop has five legs; SM-1's "Validates" list covers confirmed plan, grounded study, assessment and recovery (FR-1, FR-7, FR-8, FR-10–FR-14, FR-21) but omits FR-15 — the "spaced return" leg — even though SM-1's own pilot protocol includes a "seven-day delayed check" phase that exercises it (and the addendum §F pilot summary confirms reviews are touched). The instructor arc fares worse: UJ-4 is a full journey, but SM-2 verifies only its boundary behavior (access/approval/consent/deletion), and no metric functionally verifies that authoring works (FR-3) or that participation tracking displays (FR-16). Given the PRD's own frame — "Metrics below are functional-verification metrics" (§7) — this is not a C5 limitation; verifying an authoring workflow needs no second participant.

### Findings

- **medium** Six FRs claimed by no success metric (§7) — FR-3, FR-6, FR-15, FR-16, FR-19 and FR-20 appear in no "Validates" list (verified against all four SMs). The set includes a thesis leg (FR-15) and the instructor functional arc (FR-3/FR-16), while the boundary halves of those same features are covered — the split looks like list-completeness drift, not a designed deferral. *Fix:* add FR-15 to SM-1's Validates list (its seven-day delayed-check phase already exercises it), and either add one SM for the instructor authoring→enrollment→approval→reporting loop or state explicitly in §7 that FR-3/FR-6/FR-16/FR-19/FR-20 integrated verification lives in D7's milestone quality gates (§11) with a pointer.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This is the PRD's best dimension and downstream story creation can lean on it. Every FR carries a "Consequences (testable)" block, and the checks are boundary-encoding, not adjectives: "79.96% does not pass an 80% mark even if display rounds to 80.0%" (FR-13); "timeout never means success" and "Generation completes or presents a retryable failure/pending outcome with saved input by 120 seconds" (FR-1); "a disabled placeholder does not satisfy this FR" (FR-17); "Hard self-report can bring a review forward, never postpone one or award credit" (FR-15); "password-protected PDF is rejected with an honest failure message — never a silent 'extraction succeeded'" (UJ-1 edge, restated in FR-1). Non-functional sections carry bounds, not vibes: the accessibility bar is "keyboard operable, named controls, visible focus … at 360-pixel and desktop widths without horizontal overflow" with WCAG 2.2 AA as target-not-certification (§8.2); async operations get the 120-second rule; performance/cost budgets are the one deliberate absence, fenced as a G6 gate with a `[NOTE FOR PM]` rather than invented. No instances of "handles X gracefully," "reasonable performance" or "user-friendly" found. The single soft spot is inheritance-level: a few checks are only as testable as the initiative language they quote, and one of those never gets an operational anchor anywhere in the PRD or addendum.

### Findings

- **low** FR-8's "suitable treatment" has no operational anchor (§4.2) — the consequence "The same objective yields suitable treatment for a non-CS beginner vs a CS graduate" inherits R8's wording but leaves "suitable" to the grader's eye; unlike FR-9 ("representative" → operationalized by SM-3's "no placeholders"), nothing downstream pins what counts. *Fix:* anchor it, e.g. "background-differentiated variants exist for the same objective, factual correctness and instructor-required outcomes are unchanged (checked against the D8 reference rubric), and the pilot records which variant was delivered."

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit, tagged and indexed. §5 is a Non-Goals section doing real work — ten exclusions, each with its policy cite (C1, C6, D5, D6, G2), several of which preempt silent assumptions (no email/SMS/push, no OCR, no efficacy claims "from the pilot or any planning artifact (C5/C6, AD-18)"). De-scoping is proposed honestly and reversibly: §6.2 defers instructor workflows and breadth to milestones 2–4 with "*Deferred, not dropped (C3)*" — matching the initiative's no-silent-scope-shedding constraint. FR-level omissions are flagged inline ("**Out of Scope:** image-heavy scanned material (no OCR)" under FR-1; FR-22's "any claim of present compliance — these are targets to verify"). The Assumptions Index (§12) covers the PRD's genuine inferences — working title, invented protagonists, the MVP→D7-milestone-1 mapping, SM-target proposals, the §8.4 synthesis. Open-items density is appropriate to the stakes: 7 open questions + 6 indexed assumptions + 1 PM note on a document that explicitly "authorizes nothing" (§0) — the open items are external gates (provider terms, Node release, fixture rights), not undecided product shape. The two roundtrip loose ends found are mechanical, not structural (see tail).

## Shape fit — strong

Right shape for what it is: a chain-top, course-submission-rigor PRD for a product with real consumer shape (learners + instructors, meaningful UX), so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing — and are used (§4 section descriptions and individual FRs cite "Realizes UJ-N"). It is neither over-formalized (no gratuitous persona roster; metrics stay within what C5 permits — functional verification, explicitly "not effectiveness or demand") nor under-formalized (no consumer product arriving UJ-less). The course-specific obligations are accommodated without distorting the document: FR-19 (the AI Fluency 4D presentation deliverable) is carried as a requirement while being honest that it is "Not a mandatory in-product 4D assessment feature." The two-document structure (PRD + addendum) fits the stated consumer — the addendum self-describes as "a citable digest, not a second source of truth," keeping research depth available without bloating the PRD or creating dueling requirement sources.

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index roundtrip — two loose ends.** (1) The §12 index entry "§2.2 — Non-user list (v1) is this PRD's synthesis, not a settled decision" has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag at §2.2 — the tag exists only in the index. (2) The §7 preamble promises "targets marked [ASSUMPTION] are this PRD's proposals," but no SM carries an inline `[ASSUMPTION]` marker; the §12 §7 entry is thus unanchored in the section it indexes. Both one-line fixes (add the tag at §2.2; either tag the proposed targets inline, e.g. SM-3's 3/3 and 6/6, or reword the preamble).
- **Glossary drift (hyphenation).** Glossary and FR-10 use "Needs practice" / "Review due" (§3 lines on roadmap node, Needs practice; FR-10 consequences), while FR-20 uses hyphenated "Needs-practice / Review-due indicators." Compound-adjective use explains it, but the PRD's own rule is "Terms are used verbatim … synonyms are discipline violations" (§3) — normalize one way.
- **FR source-citation phrasing.** FR-7 "(source R2's setup sibling R7; C2)" and FR-22 "(source R2's lifecycle sibling R22; D5)" describe R7/R22 as siblings of R2; the initiative decision log shows R21/R22 were added as their own requirements ("R1–R20 retain their IDs; R21/R22 cover setup confirmation and data lifecycle") and R7 sources from the brief's Learning-experience section, not R2. The R-IDs themselves are correct and the 1:1 mapping holds; only the "sibling" framing could mislead a provenance trace. Suggest plain "(source R7; C2)".
- **ID continuity — clean.** All 22 FRs unique and present; FR presentation order is thematic (epic-grouped: FR-1, FR-2, FR-7, FR-21, FR-22 …) rather than numeric, which is deliberate and consistent with addendum §D's table; UJ/SM/SM-C namespaces distinct; no broken cross-references found. Required sections all present for the agreed stakes; addendum §E correctly distinguishes pre-existing UX state IDs (U0–U8) from the PRD's new UJ IDs.
