# Product policy baseline

On 2026-10-21 the user requested “decide all for me” during requirements refinement. These are **assistant-selected, reversible product defaults under that delegation**, not individually user-selected settings or scientifically established thresholds. They supplement the [initiative](initiative-ai-learning-platform.md); earlier explicit user decisions prevail. The approved brief and adopted discovery addendum remain historical inputs, with their open/proposal wording superseded only by the decisions here.

This is a requirements baseline for planning, not authorization to implement, deploy, process real data or run the pilot. The later explicit shared-architecture request authorizes stack/target-provider planning, recorded in the [adopted architecture](architecture-shared-technical/architecture-shared-technical.md); it does not authorize accounts, purchases/contracts or activate a processor. Numeric limits below are acceptance targets, not verified current capabilities.

## D1 — Learning structure and completion

- **Hierarchy:** a personal learning path or an instructor-managed course groups mini-courses; a mini-course contains lessons, content items, activities and related tests. Every roadmap node represents one entire mini-course.
- **Progress:** nodes show Not started, In progress or Completed. Needs practice and Review due are separate indicators, not replacements for completion. A parent path/course completes when all its required mini-courses complete.
- **Navigation:** learners can browse all available mini-courses in their personal path or enrolled, published instructor course. Recommend prerequisite order, but do not hard-lock nodes. Unpublished, unapproved or unenrolled content remains inaccessible; no XP or rewards are required.
- **Content finished:** explicitly mark each required reading/media item done and submit each required non-test activity. Both are necessary where both exist. No minimum dwell time, scroll tracking or forced full playback; optional enrichment is clearly labeled and does not block completion.
- **Tests remain separate:** all tests attached as course-completion requirements must pass under D3. Explicitly labeled unscored practice is not a required test and never silently substitutes for one. Marking content done alone does not award a pass.
- **Version scope:** completion belongs to the mini-course version studied. New drafts or added optional material do not erase prior completion. Learners remain on their published version until explicitly choosing a new version; changed required work is shown before switching. Review failure does not revoke completion. Erroneous evidence follows D3/D4's correction process.
- **Checks:** reading marked done but required activity missing; content finished but test missing/failed; normal pass; targeted-recovery pass; parent with incomplete child; later review due/failure; new version without silent progress migration.

## D2 — Setup, personalization and evidence

- **Short setup:** infer candidate themes from uploaded material, let the learner confirm/correct them, and offer Exam preparation, Understand a topic, Apply a skill and Other. Other accepts short input; only follow-ups that change the plan are required. Exam date is conditional, not universal; background/time preferences are optional unless essential to the selected goal.
- **Plan before full generation:** for personal learning, preview editable outcomes, mini-courses/lessons, practice, estimated effort, source coverage and missing explanations. Generation follows confirmation. In instructor courses, learners personalize explanations/support, not published required outcomes or assessments; instructors edit those through authoring and approval.
- **Optional starting check:** offer 3–5 diagnostic questions; allow skipping without penalty. Neither skipping nor passing grants a completion exemption. Unassessed objectives remain Unknown, not Weak.
- **Gap guidance:** connect a recommendation to the relevant response/objective and explain uncertainty. Use Needs practice for observed difficulty; do not assert whether its exact cause is forgetting, misunderstanding or a bad item. Allow the learner to flag an unclear question.
- **Evidence separation:** keep unaided submitted performance, assisted practice, activity/completion and optional Easy/Unsure/Hard self-report distinguishable. Self-report never awards a pass or proves mastery; Hard may bring review forward under D6.
- **Checks:** corrected inferred topic; editable personal plan; instructor-required outcomes unchanged by learner edits; skipped diagnostic; confident but incorrect answer; unknown objective; assisted response not mislabeled as unaided evidence.

## D3 — Assessment and recovery

- **Use:** formative learning and practice, not certified credentials, proctored exams or high-stakes academic grades.
- **Pass mark:** retain the user-selected **80% default**, configurable by instructors for their courses. Show threshold, available points and scoring criteria before starting. Compare the unrounded earned-points percentage with the threshold; display one decimal and an explicit Pass/Not passed status. For example, 79.96% does not pass an 80% mark even if rounded display reads 80.0%.
- **Scoring:** objective items use declared answer rules. Partial credit is allowed only where a disclosed rubric defines it; no negative marking. Subjective instructor-course AI grading is Provisional until the instructor confirms it and cannot award completion while pending. Personal-study AI grading is labeled as AI-evaluated and can be challenged.
- **Attempt integrity:** snapshot the content, test, rubric and pass-mark versions when an attempt starts. Later threshold/rubric changes apply only to new attempts, never retrospectively revoke an earned pass. Show previous attempts rather than silently overwriting them. Scored attempts withhold answers until submission; hints/assistance belong to separately labeled practice, not unaided scored evidence.
- **Retries:** no pedagogical attempt limit by default. Review/reassessment is offered, not compulsory. Service quotas may delay new generation with a clear message and saved work, but do not record a failed attempt or promise unlimited free generation.
- **Targeted credit:** preserve passed sections from an originally failed test. A new retest covers the still-failed objective groups using fresh questions at the same intended cognitive demand and comparable rubric/points coverage. Each reassessed target group must reach the applicable pass mark to clear that group. Still-failed or untested recovery targets keep that failed-test requirement unsatisfied. Do not invent a higher original numeric score; show the original result and subsequent recovery credit separately.
- **No perfect-score requirement:** an ordinary test that passes at its applicable threshold needs no mandatory remediation of every wrong answer. The still-failed-area rule above applies to recovery of a test that did not pass, not to converting every passing test into a 100% requirement.
- **Quality/correction:** question variants must have valid answers and assess the stated objective; matching a label alone does not prove equivalent difficulty. A flagged item is marked Under review. If its validity could affect a pending/unearned completion decision, retain prior evidence, pause that decision and offer a corrected/replacement assessment without counting the dispute as another failure. Already-earned passes/completion are never revoked for an invalid-item finding: preserve history, annotate the evidence defect and offer optional corrected practice. Instructor adjudication applies to pending instructor-course cases; personal-study cases offer a documented corrected replacement rather than letting an unchecked AI appeal award a pass.
- **Checks:** below/equal/above threshold, rounding boundary, partial credit, pending grade, mid-attempt policy edit, repeated submission, successful/failed/partial-target retest, preserved passed sections, invalid question and ordinary 80%-passing test with wrong answers.

## D4 — Sources, deeper explanations and instructor approval

- **Source grounding:** use uploaded materials first for scope; fill missing explanations with identified supplements. Prefer relevant official specifications, primary documentation or credible educational references. Show source identity/location and distinguish uploads from supplements; check that cited passages actually support the claim. An AI-generated citation or another model's agreement is not verification.
- **Conflicts:** display conflicting claims and their sources; do not silently rewrite the upload. Instructor-course conflict resolution requires instructor approval. In personal study, explain the competing evidence and uncertainty; if no defensible answer exists, stop short of an authoritative answer and do not generate a scored item from it.
- **Why exploration:** learner requests the next depth, can stop at any point, and sees purposes, assumptions, trade-offs and limits where supported. Stop or state uncertainty when evidence runs out; no invented single “root cause” or historical origin.
- **Approval unit:** the instructor personally inspects and approves exact versions of learner-facing AI lessons/explanations, supplementary sources and assessment bundles, including questions, answers, rubric and generated feedback. Batch approval is permitted only after inspecting the listed versions, never for unseen future output. Record approver, time and approved versions.
- **Dynamic content:** assemble tailored tests from already-approved question/feedback bundles where possible. A newly generated question, feedback variant or deeper-why explanation enters a pending queue until the instructor personally approves it. Show existing approved material or an honest Pending instructor approval state meanwhile. No generation-on-demand bypass of R5.
- **Changes:** editing a source, lesson or rubric invalidates approval of affected new drafts/dependencies, not the immutable previously published version. Attempts remain pinned. Instructors may withdraw an erroneous published item: prevent new use, identify affected evidence and use D3 correction, preserving already-earned passes/completion while pausing only pending decisions. Never silently change past answers/results.
- **Personal study:** no instructor gate is imposed, but identify AI-generated/unreviewed material and retain source/error-report visibility. This is not a correctness guarantee.
- **Checks:** unsupported or fabricated citation; conflicting upload/reference; exhausted explanation; stale dependent approval; fresh tailored test pending; approved-bank retest; unapproved AI feedback denied; withdrawn item correction.

## D5 — Materials, access and data lifecycle

- **Material inputs:** initially accept text-based PDF, DOCX, TXT and Markdown, at most **20 MiB per file and ten files per generation**. Preview extracted text and extraction warnings before use. Reject unreadable/password-protected/unsupported content without claiming successful extraction. No automatic OCR, archive unpacking or remote-URL import in the initial input workflow; required learning images remain supported as authored content.
- **Student import:** UTF-8 CSV containing name and email; preview row errors and duplicates before confirmation. Invite records without accounts and associate them only with verified accounts. No imported passwords or grades; existing enrollment is not duplicated. Do not expose the roster to learners or send it to generation providers.
- **Roles:** learners control their personal study; an instructor controls their own course content/enrollment/reporting. Admin assigns instructor capability and manages account/system metadata, not ordinary access to personal-upload contents. No self-service privilege escalation or content-browsing admin feature. Log privileged operations without recording private document/answer text; any exceptional infrastructure access requires a separate, audited operational process before real-data deployment.
- **Enrollment:** course owner adds/removes learners after preview/confirmation. No public self-enrollment into restricted courses. Removal immediately ends future course-content access and its reminders; a learner may export their own progress/scores, not the restricted question bank or other students' information. Revocation does not silently delete the instructor's legitimate course records; archive/deletion rules below still apply.
- **Processing:** private visibility is not a promise that infrastructure never processes data. Before external AI processing, identify provider, purpose, data categories and retention; require informed opt-in. Refusal sends no data externally. Minimize excerpts; exclude identifiers/rosters where unnecessary. No training reuse. A provider must meet the non-training and bounded/deletable-retention requirements before real data is allowed; selection and contractual verification remain a release gate, not an assumed capability.
- **Owner controls:** authenticated owners can export or delete their personal materials and associated generated courses, extracted content, search/index representations, conversations, evidence and reminders. Warn about the deletion scope; do not delete another user's instructor-owned records through a personal-delete action. Withdrawal of processing consent stops future external processing and offers deletion of existing personal data; it cannot undo earlier disclosed processing.
- **Deletion targets:** immediately revoke access/use and stop reminders; purge active copies within **seven days**, including provider copies where retained. Backups expire within **thirty days** and deletion must be reapplied before restored data can become accessible. Do not silently substitute a provider with incompatible retention. Content-free operational/security metadata expires after thirty days. These are product targets to test, not a present compliance claim.
- **Retention:** keep study data while active unless its owner deletes it. Instructor-course records default to deletion ninety days after archive, with notice/export before deletion; personal study is not deleted merely because an unrelated instructor course archives. An institution requiring different retention/academic-record rules needs an explicit policy change before use, not a claim of institutional compliance.
- **Trust boundary:** uploaded instructions, retrieved text and generated output cannot authorize role changes, reveal another person's data or execute code. User coding activities run only in an isolated, resource-bounded execution environment; sandbox selection/testing is a mandatory implementation gate.
- **Checks:** misleading file extension/unsafe document; extraction failure; duplicate CSV row; unverified invite; cross-owner/course access; self-promotion; revoked enrollment; declined consent; identifier minimization; dependent-data purge; backup restore; reminder cancellation and restricted export.

## D6 — Review, reminders and reporting

- **Review schedule:** after completion, first review is due in one day. After each successful unaided review, use the next interval of **3, 7, 14, then 30 days**, and repeat thirty-day intervals thereafter. Intervals run from the actual successful review, not the original completion date. This is a transparent starting heuristic, not a scientifically optimal schedule.
- **Adaptation:** a failed or assisted review offers targeted support and a next-day review, restarting the interval sequence after subsequent unaided success. Hard self-report can bring the next review to the next day; self-report cannot postpone a due review, award credit or extend the interval. Review uses the applicable pass mark but cannot revoke course completion.
- **Delivery:** initially use an in-app due-review inbox and badge. At most one new consolidated reminder per local calendar day, combining overdue items; no email, SMS or push integration. The due list persists even when no new reminder is created. The default timezone is detected and changeable; due dates are visible and update predictably with timezone changes.
- **Controls:** pause reminders, snooze until tomorrow or three days later, and disable reminders. Snoozing changes notification timing, not assessment evidence; resume shows the current due list without a backlog burst. Removing enrollment/deleting a course stops its reminders.
- **Instructor report:** show enrolled learner, last activity, required-content/activity completion, attempt/pass/recovery status and due/overdue reviews for that instructor's course. Estimated active time, if shown, is explicitly an estimate based on active sessions, not background-tab dwell time or proof of learning. Exclude personal-upload/private-study evidence and other instructors' rosters.
- **Checks:** timezone/day boundary, duplicate delivery, overdue aggregation, snooze/pause/resume/disable, delayed review, assisted/failed review, confidence-performance disagreement, preserved completion and instructor-report access boundaries.

## D7 — Delivery order and quality gates

These are planning milestones, not created epics or authorization to build. No named team member or delivery date is invented.

| Order | Deliverable | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Personal-material learning loop: identity/private access, safe extraction, short setup/plan, roadmap, sourced lesson/why, active recall, scoring/recovery and in-app review | Complete permitted and denied-access journeys, normal and recovered completion, plus a clock-controlled review/reminder check |
| 2 | Instructor authoring, editing, CSV import/enrollment, exact-version approval and participation reporting | Enrolled and denied access, unpublished/generated-content denial, approved-bank/pending retests and scoped report checks |
| 3 | Assignment breadth: all three subject areas, all required format families, other-domain and learner-directed generation | Functioning representative activities across text/images, simulations, role play/mock interviews, mini-games, narrated animation/slides and isolated online coding; no inert placeholders |
| 4 | Integrated validation and final presentation | Execute D8 after functional gates; record limitations and project examples for all four AI Fluency competencies |

- **Access/approval bar:** all chosen positive/negative owner, role, enrollment and exact-version approval checks pass. Zero unresolved critical/high security defects or material completion/grading defects in the selected release scope; do not lower the bar by suppressing checks.
- **Content/assessment bar:** manually check every cited factual claim and answer/rubric in the bounded demo/pilot fixture against its source. Unsupported or materially incorrect scored items cannot ship in that fixture. Verify threshold, rounding, attempt version, provisional-grade and targeted-credit cases; generated variants require equivalent checks, not merely fluent text.
- **Usability bar:** core flows work by keyboard, have named controls, visible focus, readable contrast and error/status feedback at 360-pixel and desktop widths without page-level horizontal overflow. Narrated learning includes text equivalents; motion is not required to access the teaching content. WCAG 2.2 AA is the implementation target, not a certification claim.
- **Failure behavior:** generation/extraction shows progress and, by 120 seconds, completes or presents a retryable failure/pending outcome with saved input; timeout never means success. Retrying must not double-count scores/progress. Actual latency, quotas, cost and load budgets require implementation evidence before release, not invented benchmark results.
- **Release readiness:** verify actual processing/retention guarantees, deletion/restore behavior, authentication/access enforcement and code isolation. If not feasible, block real-data/execution use and record a revised decision; do not silently weaken privacy, approval or assignment scope.

## D8 — One-participant pilot

- **Selected case:** personal-upload journey; software-engineering unit-testing fundamentals. Goal: distinguish unit from integration tests and apply Arrange–Act–Assert to a small pure function. Use a bounded legally usable, attributable text fixture; the exact artifact/license must be verified before execution. No material is requested now; TCP remains illustrative, not the pilot.
- **Protocol:** three fresh unaided baseline recall/application items, one mini-course, its required test and natural recovery when failures occur. A thirty-minute first-session budget is a usability target, not a participant-exclusion rule. If no failure occurs, test recovery separately with a labeled scripted case, not a manufactured learning deficit.
- **Delayed check:** seven days later, attempt fresh items matched to the same objectives before viewing review/answers. Check the in-app reminder and record earlier practice/reviews as confounders. A delayed check must not be mislabeled as unpractised retention if intervening reviews occurred.
- **Evidence:** save responses, scores/rubric judgments, assistance, attempts, friction and source/grading problems. Use a reference/rubric checked independently of the generating model; the single participant's own checking still has bias. Report observed differences descriptively, with no required improvement percentage, causal effectiveness, comparative superiority or PMF claim.
- **Separate functional checks:** learner/instructor/non-enrolled test accounts verify access, reporting and pending-content denial; they are not additional study participants or instructor-usability validation. Early review scheduling can use a controlled clock for functional tests; the delayed pilot observation uses real elapsed time.
- **Go/no-go:** run only after D7 gates pass. An inability to finish, missing reminder, broken recovery, unsupported explanation or material scoring/privacy defect becomes a recorded issue to fix, not hidden by declaring the pilot successful. Broader demand research remains deferred.

## Remaining implementation and release gates

- **Architecture:** the adopted spine selects shared technical planning contracts. Verify its G1–G6 runtime/provider/hosting/retention/isolation and resource gates before affected implementation/activation; no external account, purchase or contract is authorized here. These technical choices do not weaken D1–D8.
- **Delivery planning:** six epics assign coverage/dependencies; private-study-setup is refined with thirteen planned entries plus UX/feasibility companions, while five remain unrefined. No application work, capacity commitment or live-use permission is created. Refine remaining epics and clear explicit start/release gates before scheduling/building.
- **Pilot readiness:** obtain a rights-checked fixture and working reference rubric before executing D8; no invented evidence or uploads now.
- **Policy changes:** preserve explicit user choices. If feasibility, institutional obligations or evidence requires changing one of these defaults, record the change and rationale rather than silently changing the baseline.
