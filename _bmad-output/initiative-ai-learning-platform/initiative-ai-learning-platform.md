# AI Learning Platform

## Description

Create a web-first, GenAI-powered learning platform that turns learner or instructor inputs into personalized mini-courses, helps learners identify what needs attention, and supports deeper understanding, active recall and spaced review. The product brief was approved on 2026-10-20; the user delegated remaining product-policy decisions on 2026-10-21. This requirements baseline includes the assistant-selected defaults in the policy companion, not permission to build. Risk is provisionally high because AI content/assessment quality and private data access require verification.

## Outcome

Learners can study suitable material, complete its content and pass related tests, receive focused support for failed questions, and return for scheduled review; instructors control student-facing AI content. An initial one-participant pilot can reveal workflow and content problems, but does not establish general learning effectiveness or product–market fit.

## Requirements

Each stable ID names a capability and an observable acceptance check. Source labels refer to References. D1–D8 identify assistant-selected defaults in the product-policy companion, chosen under the user's 2026-10-21 delegation; they are not claims that the user individually selected each policy. Implementation/release gates remain explicit.

- **R1 — Personal-material learning** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; Product and intended value; delegated D5): learners can upload text-based PDF, DOCX, TXT or Markdown and obtain a mini-course after extraction preview and confirmed setup. Verify a personal-upload-to-study path and unreadable/unsupported/oversized-file failures; D5 sets input limits and safe failure behavior.
- **R2 — Private uploads** (brief: Content, access and instructor control): personal uploads are visible only to their uploading learner. Verify owner access and denial to other learner/instructor accounts, including direct attempts to access the upload.
- **R3 — Instructor authoring** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; overview: instructor objective; delegated D2/D4): instructors can upload/edit teaching material, manually or with AI customize existing content/activities, and generate/refine new mini-courses from topics, requirements and teaching content. Demonstrate creation and iterative editing with instructor-controlled outcomes, versioned drafts and affected reapproval rather than silent published-content replacement.
- **R4 — Student import and enrollment access** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; overview: instructor objective; delegated D5): course owners import students using previewed/confirmed name-and-email CSV and manage enrollment. Demonstrate verified invited/enrolled student access, duplicate/error handling, denied non-enrolled access and immediate access/reminder revocation after removal, with restricted history export under D5.
- **R5 — Instructor approval before student use** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; delegated D4): the instructor personally approves exact versions of learner-visible AI lessons/explanations, tests/answer/rubric/feedback bundles and supplementary sources. Verify unapproved or stale-draft content is unavailable, including fresh tailored retests and why responses; use approved content or wait for approval, never approve unseen future output. Demonstrate approver/version records, dependency reapproval and withdrawal/correction handling.
- **R6 — Supplement missing explanations** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; delegated D4): if uploads lack a necessary explanation, supplement with identified, claim-supporting external sources. Demonstrate source locations, checked support, visible upload/reference conflicts and unsupported-answer handling without silent replacement; R5 applies in instructor courses.
- **R7 — Selection-first goals** (brief: Learning experience and progress; delegated D2): after upload, learners select Exam preparation, Understand a topic, Apply a skill or Other and answer only critical conditional follow-ups, without long purpose essays. Demonstrate short-choice setup, a short Other route and no universal exam-date requirement; R21 covers confirmation, plan preview and the optional starting check.
- **R8 — Background and goal adaptation** (brief: Product and intended value; overview: personalization objective; delegated D2): tailor explanations and support to different backgrounds/goals while preserving factual correctness and instructor-required outcomes. Demonstrate suitable treatment of the same objective for a non-CS beginner and a CS graduate without learner edits weakening published course requirements.
- **R9 — Required subject coverage** (brief: Course obligations and decisions for later; overview: topic objectives): support mini-courses covering software-engineering/development fundamentals, effective use of AI coding agents, and soft/product skills. Verify representative learning content and activities in all three areas, not only one pilot topic.
- **R10 — Gaps and next-study guidance** (brief: Product and intended value; Learning experience and progress; delegated D2): identify knowledge needing attention and explain what to study next from relevant response/objective evidence. Demonstrate useful guidance, uncertainty and unknown-versus-observed-difficulty distinctions; separate assisted practice, unaided performance, self-report and engagement rather than claim certain diagnosis.
- **R11 — Deeper why exploration** (brief: Learning experience and progress; delegated D4): learners can successively request deeper explanations and stop at any depth. Demonstrate supported purposes, assumptions or trade-offs beyond restatement, source visibility and honest stopping when evidence is exhausted, subject to R5. TCP remains illustrative; D8 selects a different pilot.
- **R12 — Active recall** (brief: Product and intended value): provide tasks in which learners attempt retrieval before viewing the answer, followed by feedback. Demonstrate attempt-before-answer behavior rather than rereading alone.
- **R13 — Course completion and pass marks** (brief: Learning experience and progress; user decisions: 2026-10-20/21; delegated D1/D3): finish required content items and activities and pass all related required tests, normally or through R14, before completing a mini-course; complete every required mini-course before completing its parent. Retain the **80% default** for learner-generated and instructor courses, instructor-configurable for their courses; compare unrounded scores at or above the applicable mark using attempt-snapshotted policies. Verify required-item/activity gaps, provisional/failed/unattempted tests, threshold/rounding boundaries, passing with some wrong answers, version changes and normal/recovered completion without retroactive loss of earned progress.
- **R14 — Targeted recovery and reassessment** (brief: Learning experience and progress; user decision: 2026-10-21; delegated D3/D4): inspect failed questions, offer focused review, then a fresh same-objective/demand retest. Passing a targeted group clears that failed area toward the original failed-test requirement without repeating passed sections or inventing a new original score; uncleared recovery targets still block that requirement. Verify successful/failed/partial-target recovery, retained passes, disclosed rubrics, unlimited pedagogical retries with honest resource delays, disputed-item correction and R5 approval. D3 defines scoring, version and equivalence checks without demanding perfect scores on already-passed tests.
- **R15 — Spaced review and reminders** (brief: Learning experience and progress; delegated D6): schedule reviews using initial 1/3/7/14/30-day intervals, adjusted by unaided review results, with an in-app due list and at most one consolidated reminder per local day. Verify time-gap return, failure/assistance and Hard-feedback adaptation, timezone behavior, pause/snooze/disable and overdue aggregation without erasing course completion; D6 defines the heuristic and controls, not an optimal-retention claim.
- **R16 — Instructor participation tracking** (brief: Content, access and instructor control; overview: instructor objective; delegated D6): show enrolled learner, last activity, required-item progress, attempt/pass/recovery status and due/overdue reviews for the instructor's course; label active-time estimates if shown. Verify scoped reports and denied cross-course/personal-study access, without equating engagement with mastery.
- **R17 — Interactive format coverage** (brief: Course obligations and decisions for later; overview: formats objective): support text/images, interactive simulations, role play/mock interviews, games/mini-games, AI-narrated animations/slides and online coding. Verify functioning representative activities across these format families, not placeholders; every lesson need not use every format.
- **R18 — Other-domain and self-directed learning** (brief: Product and intended value; overview: open-domain objective): users can generate mini-courses outside the main domain, and learners can generate their own topics and self-learning paths. Demonstrate another-domain topic and learner-directed path without making instructor courses public.
- **R19 — AI Fluency presentation deliverable** (brief: Course obligations and decisions for later; overview: final paragraph): the final presentation explains how Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence apply to the project, with project-specific examples for all four. This is not a mandatory in-product 4D assessment feature.
- **R20 — Mini-course roadmap nodes** (brief: Learning experience and progress; user decision: 2026-10-20; delegated D1): after goal selection, show entire-mini-course nodes as Not started, In progress or Completed, with separate Needs practice/Review due indicators. Verify R13 completion mapping, recommended prerequisite order and free browsing of available authorized nodes without hard locks, XP or loss of completion when review becomes due.
- **R21 — Confirmable generation setup** (delegated D2): learners confirm/correct inferred topic and goal, edit a personal course plan before full generation, and may take or skip a 3–5-question starting check. Verify corrected-plan and skipped-check paths without completion exemptions or changes to instructor-required outcomes.
- **R22 — Processing and data lifecycle controls** (delegated D5): disclose and obtain opt-in before external processing, minimize provider data and support authenticated owner export/deletion of personal material and its derivatives. Verify declined-consent denial, owner-only controls, immediate access/use/reminder revocation, seven-day active-copy purge, thirty-day backup expiry/restore suppression and ninety-day post-archive instructor-course deletion with notice/export. Provider/institution compatibility must be verified before real use; no current compliance is claimed.

## Constraints

- **C1 — Web first** (brief: Product and intended value): the first delivery form is web; no stack or infrastructure is selected by these requirements.
- **C2 — Low-writing setup** (brief: Learning experience and progress): selection-first goals and only critical questions; do not require long purpose descriptions unless necessary.
- **C3 — Preserve full assignment scope** (brief: Course obligations and decisions for later; overview): a focused delivery slice or pilot must not silently remove required subjects, activity families, instructor workflows or other-domain/self-learning scope. Delivery priorities require user agreement.
- **C4 — No access or approval shortcuts** (brief: Content, access and instructor control): privacy, enrolled-only access and personal instructor approval still apply during personalization, supplementation and tailored reassessment.
- **C5 — Limited initial validation** (brief: Initial validation and evidence limits): the project initiator is the sole initial participant; broader demand/current-alternative discovery is deferred. Select material and protocol before validation, not as a prerequisite for requirements. Do not infer causal effectiveness or PMF from one self-test.
- **C6 — Formative scope and evidence integrity** (delegated D2/D3): do not represent the product as certified/high-stakes grading, infer permanent mastery from activity/completion, or conflate assisted practice/self-report with unaided performance. Policy edits and invalid-item findings cannot revoke earned passes/completion; annotate defective evidence and offer correction, pausing only pending/unearned decisions.
- **C7 — Delivery and release gates** (delegated D5/D7/D8): follow the selected milestone order without dropping required scope; demonstrate access/approval, assessment/version/recovery, source/rubric, accessibility and honest error-handling checks before the pilot. Verify actual provider/retention, cost and isolated-code-execution feasibility before real use. The later explicit architecture request authorizes stack/target-provider planning only; no implementation, account, contract or deployment authorization is implied.

## Done when

These are initiative-level end checks, not claims that implementation or validation has happened:

1. A web learner can complete personal input, short goal setup and plan confirmation, skip or take the optional diagnostic, see whole-mini-course roadmap nodes, use suitable learning/practice, and satisfy R13's content-plus-tests rule; failure and targeted recovery are demonstrable (R1, R7–R14, R20–R21).
2. Private-upload, enrollment, instructor-approval and processing/lifecycle boundaries hold in permitted and denied scenarios, including supplementary content, tailored tests, consent refusal and export/deletion (R2–R6, R22).
3. Scheduled reviews and reminders support a return after a time gap; instructors can inspect course participation without access to private learner uploads (R15–R16).
4. Required subject areas, interactive format families and other-domain/self-learning paths are demonstrated, rather than removed to simplify the pilot (R9, R17–R18).
5. The one-participant unit-testing pilot follows D8 after readiness/quality gates, with observations and limitations recorded; completion of the pilot is not a positive-effectiveness or demand claim (C5, C7).
6. Final-presentation material explains all four AI Fluency competencies using project-specific examples (R19).
